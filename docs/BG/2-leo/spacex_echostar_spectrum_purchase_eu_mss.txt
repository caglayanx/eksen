Does SpaceX's $17 billion spectrum purchase from EchoStar include the company's European 2 GHz MSS authorisations? SpaceX claims that the deal involves “global” MSS spectrum licences. However, it’s not clear if that includes the European ones. In Europe, EchoStar—alongside Viasat—is one of only two holders of an EU-wide MSS authorisation.

In EchoStar’s filings with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), only the US spectrum rights are explicitly mentioned. However, there is also mention of “certain international authorisations, filings, concessions, licenses, rights, and priorities related to that spectrum and certain assets associated therewith (collectively, the ‘Foreign Assets’)”.

If the deal does include European assets, this could be significant because these authorisations are set to expire in 2027. The European Commission is still considering what will happen after this and has yet to announce a clear plan.

Some experts assume that EchoStar’s licence will be revoked. The company has long been accused of not using its spectrum, a claim it has consistently denied. Yet if SpaceX does take over these rights, any argument in favour of revoking them becomes much weaker.

Either way, this decision will have major implications for the European 2 GHz MSS plan, which is becoming the backbone of D2D operations around the world.
